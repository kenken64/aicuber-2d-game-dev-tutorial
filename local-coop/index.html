HOW LOCAL CO-OP WORKS

One world, two input streams.

Both players stay on the same screen. The keyboard only moves Player 1; the first connected gamepad only moves Player 2.

A coin belongs to the player who touches it first. The team total and both personal counters are saved for your next visit.

Forest, coin, and explorer art reused from the earlier Godot Forge game modules. Player 2 wears a blue tunic and amber cloak.

Co-op gameplay build prompt
Create a browser-based shared-screen 2D forest coin-collection lesson. Show two explorers at once, with Player 1 controlled only by A/D or arrow keys plus W/Up/Space to jump, and Player 2 controlled only by the first connected gamepad using the left stick or D-pad plus the bottom face button to jump. Keep the full playfield visible to both players. Give the players distinct clothing palettes and visible P1/P2 labels. Place a separate coin counter at the top left for Player 1 and top right for Player 2. Award each coin once to the first player who touches it. Show a shared team total, reconnect feedback, and completion when every coin is collected. Save coin ownership and player positions in a module checkpoint, and support a module-specific reset. Keep text outside the canvas where practical and make the page responsive and accessible.

Explorer sprite source art prompt
Use GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst to create a transparent-background 2D pixel-art side-scroller player sprite sheet: one brave forest explorer with short brown hair, a teal-green tunic, purple scarf, leather boots, and a clearly visible steel sword held in the right hand. Exact 8 columns × 7 rows, 56 equal 256×256 cells, one complete centered character per cell, consistent scale, baseline, proportions and orientation facing right. Rows in order: idle (one standing pose repeated across all eight cells), walk (eight distinct alternating footfall poses with crossed legs), run (eight faster poses), sword attack (four clear readable sword-swing poses; sword must be visible and part of the character in every attack frame), jump (four poses), hurt (four poses), death (four poses). Keep the complete body and sword inside each cell with generous transparent padding. Crisp readable pixel clusters, polished hand-crafted game art, consistent forest-adventure palette. True transparent alpha; no background, scenery, grid lines, text, labels, watermark, cropped objects, or extra characters.
